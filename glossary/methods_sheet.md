|---|---|---|---|---|
| **statistical analysis**<br><small>statistical analyses</small> | statistical idea | 1270 | Using math on collected numbers to find patterns. It also checks whether a difference is real or could just be luck. Imagine checking whether one class really scored higher on a test, or whether the gap is small enough to be chance. | Researchers use it to decide whether their results show a real effect. |
| **analysis of variance**<br><small>ANOVA, one-way analysis</small> | statistical method | 416 | A test that compares the averages of three or more groups at once. | **principal component analysis**<br><small>PCA</small> | statistical method | 389 | A method that squeezes many measured variables into a few new combined ones that keep most of the variation. | **correlation**<br><small>correlation analysis, correlation coefficient, negative correlation, strong correlation</small> | statistical idea | 298 | How closely two quantities rise or fall together. It is measured by a number, the correlation coefficient, which runs from -1 to +1. Positive means both go up together, like height and shoe size. Negative means one goes up as the other goes down. "Strong" means the link is close, and "significant" means it is unlikely to be just chance. | Researchers use it to see which factors are linked, such as water temperature and fish numbers. |
| **phylogenetic analysis**<br><small>phylogenetic analyses</small> | computing method | 268 | Working out how species or genes are related by comparing their DNA or features. | **sensitivity analysis** | computing method | 212 | Changing the inputs or assumptions of a model a little and watching how much the results change. | **standard deviation**<br><small>SD</small> | statistical idea | 188 | A number showing how spread out values are around their average. A small value means most values sit close to the average. A large value means they are widely scattered. | Researchers report it to show how much their measurements vary. |
| **Kruskal-Wallis test**<br><small>wallis test</small> | statistical method | 183 | A test that compares three or more groups using only the order of the values, not the exact numbers. | **Shannon index** | statistical method | 169 | A score for how diverse a community of living things is. | **Akaike information criterion**<br><small>AIC, best model, final model, full model</small> | statistical method | 169 | A score for comparing models, which are math descriptions of how something works. A "full model" includes every possible factor, and the "final model" is the one researchers keep. | Researchers use it to decide which factors best explain their results without overcomplicating things. |
| **polymerase chain reaction**<br><small>PCR, PCR amplification</small> | lab technique | 158 | A lab method that copies a chosen piece of DNA millions of times. It works like a photocopier for genes. | Researchers use it to get enough DNA to detect, identify or sequence organisms. |
| **agarose gel electrophoresis** | lab technique | 150 | A method that sorts DNA pieces by size. | **scanning electron microscopy**<br><small>SEM</small> | instrument | 150 | A microscope that scans a beam of electrons, the tiny charged particles found in atoms, across a surface instead of using light. It gives very detailed, 3D-looking pictures of tiny things. | Researchers use it to view fine surface details of cells, pollen, minerals or particles. |
| **maximum likelihood**<br><small>ML</small> | statistical method | 134 | A way of choosing the explanation or settings that make the observed data most probable. | **mass spectrometry**<br><small>MS analysis</small> | instrument | 117 | A method that turns molecules into charged particles and sorts them by weight. | **amplicon sequence variants**<br><small>ASVs</small> | computing method | 116 | The exact, distinct DNA sequences found after copying and reading one gene from a mixed sample. | **limit of detection**<br><small>LOD</small> | statistical idea | 113 | The smallest amount of a substance that a method can reliably tell apart from zero. Anything below it might be there but cannot be measured with confidence. | Researchers use it to show how sensitive their measurements are. |
| **statistical tests** | statistical idea | 113 | A statistical test is a set of math rules for deciding whether a pattern in data is probably real or could easily have happened by chance. | **receiver operating characteristic curve and area under the curve (ROC / AUC)**<br><small>ROC, AUC, area under the curve, receiver operating characteristic</small> | statistical idea | 111 | A ROC curve is a graph showing how well a model sorts things into two groups, such as places where a species lives and places where it doesn't, at every possible cut-off. | **Pearson correlation**<br><small>pearson correlation analysis, pearson correlation coefficient, strong positive correlation, significant negative correlation</small> | statistical method | 109 | Pearson correlation gives a number from -1 to 1 showing how closely two measurements rise and fall together in a straight-line way. | **remote sensing** | field method | 105 | Remote sensing means gathering information about Earth from far away, usually with cameras and sensors on satellites, planes or drones. | **random forest (RF)**<br><small>RF, random forest</small> | computing method | 102 | A random forest is a computer method that builds hundreds of simple decision trees, each a flowchart of yes/no questions, using slightly different parts of the data. | **rRNA gene sequencing**<br><small>rrna gene sequencing, 16S rRNA gene sequencing</small> | lab technique | 90 | This reads the DNA letters of a gene for rRNA, a molecule that helps cells build proteins and that all microbes have.
