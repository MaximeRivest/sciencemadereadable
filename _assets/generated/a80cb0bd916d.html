Results Across plantations and years, cone weight was on average 470 g, with an average PY of 3.91%. ## Results ### Comparing heavy and light cones Seeds in these results include their shells. Kernels are the edible parts inside the shells; the kernel counts reported here include only healthy ones. The **edible share (PY)** is the weight of dried healthy kernels as a percentage of fresh cone weight. For the results from Table 4, the number after **±** is the **standard error**. It describes uncertainty in an estimated average, not the full spread of individual measurements. For a percentage, the standard error is in percentage points. Across plantations and years, fresh cone weight averaged 473.6 ± 25.8 g, or about 474 g. The average edible share was 3.91 ± 0.16% of fresh cone weight (Table 4). We compared light cones, weighing less than 393 g, with heavy cones, weighing more than 503 g. These groups were the lightest and heaviest thirds of the cone-weight distribution; the middle third was left out of the comparison. Light cones averaged 338.0 ± 4.7 g, and heavy cones averaged 594.9 ± 5.7 g. For every measured trait, the statistical tests found no clear evidence that the heavy–light difference varied among plantations (p > 0.05). This does not prove that the pattern was identical at every plantation. Heavy cones contained more seeds and healthy kernels per cone. Their seeds and kernels were also heavier, longer and wider. The tests supported the differences shown below (Table 4).
