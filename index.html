How it works

Scientific papers are written for other scientists. This page rewrites a whole paper for a curious 14-year-old: in the authors' own voice, keeping every result, number and caveat, but in words anyone can follow.

The rewriting is done by small open models we trained (Qwen 3.5 with 9, 4 and 0.8 billion parameters). They learned from thousands of papers rewritten by frontier AI models. They are small enough to run on a gaming graphics card, and that is where they run: an RTX 3090 in my home. On busy days a rented Nvidia H100 joins it. When many people are reading, a paper may wait in line, and the page says when the GPU is offline.

How good is it? On our test papers, a judge model scored our 9B 7.8 out of 10, a little above GPT-6 Luna (7.5), below Claude Opus (8.5); it costs about half a cent per paper. It still makes mistakes more often than the big models, so check the original before relying on a detail. With your own API key you can compare with six commercial models.

Which papers? Open-access research articles in PubMed Central under the CC BY licence, which allows republishing an adapted version with credit. Figures and tables are always the original ones.

A demo, not a product. We count visits on our own server, without cookies: each visitor is an anonymous code that changes every day, and what you search for is never stored. If your browser asks not to be tracked, nothing is counted. Rewrites made by our models are kept so the next reader gets them instantly.
