Teaching a small model to take notes on science

The bundle-maker reads one section of a scientific paper and writes it down as dense, checkable notes: every number, name, citation and hedge ("may", "suggests"), nothing else. Those notes are what the small writer turns into a paper a 14-year-old can read. Today the big models (Opus, Astra) take the notes; if a 0.8B model can, the whole chain gets cheap.

Every number, citation and hedge kept: a free code check, per section; the teachers' final notes all pass it. Meaning check: Opus compares the notes with the paper (missing, changed or added information), the same check the teachers' notes pass before the writer sees them. The yardstick is the teachers' first notes, before any fixing: the small model's notes are as usable as theirs if they have about as many problems, because the same checks and fixes can follow. Opus checks 4 papers per checkpoint (about 20 sections), and all 20 papers for the best and the last.
